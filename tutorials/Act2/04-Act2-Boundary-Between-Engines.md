# 04 — The Boundary Between the Engines

The Two‑Copilot Model works because the two engines never cross into each other’s domain.  
This separation is not stylistic.  
It is the structural boundary that keeps the HCMD loop stable.

The boundary is the **STS**.

Above the boundary:

- ambiguity is allowed  
- meaning is refined  
- intent is negotiated  
- tasks are clarified  

Below the boundary:

- ambiguity is forbidden  
- structure is derived  
- implementation is generated  
- determinism is enforced  

The STS is the hinge between these two worlds.  
It is the last artifact shaped by meaning and the first artifact consumed by structure.

---

## Skills and the Boundary

Skills (Huts) belong entirely to the **Meaning‑Side Engine**.  
They form as reusable semantic units during STS refinement.

When the STS becomes deterministic, Skills cross the boundary **only as structure**:

- a Skill becomes a PSC component  
- a cluster of Skills becomes a PSC grouping  
- Skill relationships become structural dependencies  

The structure‑side engine does not modify Skills.  
It receives them as fixed units and arranges them mechanically.

This is why the boundary must remain strict:  
Skills are refined above the boundary, and **only their structural form** appears below it.

---

## Why the boundary must exist

If meaning leaks downward, the structure‑side engine receives ambiguity and produces inconsistent code.  
If implementation leaks upward, the meaning‑side engine becomes biased by structure and loses semantic clarity.

Both directions break the loop.

The boundary ensures:

- meaning stays human‑anchored  
- structure stays machine‑anchored  
- refinement happens only where it belongs  
- determinism emerges only when ambiguity is gone  

---

## How the boundary stabilizes co‑intelligence

The boundary prevents the engines from interfering with each other.  
It allows each engine to specialize:

- one in interpretation  
- one in execution  

This specialization is what makes HCMD reproducible.  
The loop works because the engines meet **only** at the STS, and nowhere else.
