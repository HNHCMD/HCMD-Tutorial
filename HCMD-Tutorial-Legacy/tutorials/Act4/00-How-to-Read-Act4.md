# 00‑How‑to‑Read‑Act4

Act4 is the point in the HCMD sequence where the system stops explaining _what_ it does and begins explaining _why it must be this way_.  
If Acts 1–3 describe the mechanics, Act4 describes the **structural philosophy** that makes those mechanics stable over time.

This document tells you how to read Act4 without getting lost in the surface details.

* * *

**1. Act4 is not a tutorial. It is a justification.**
-----------------------------------------------------

Act4 does not teach you how to run HCMD.  
It explains why HCMD is built the way it is, and why alternative designs collapse under their own ambiguity.

Every section in Act4 answers a single question:

**“What structural property must hold for HCMD to remain deterministic, reproducible, and cross‑language stable for decades?”**

If you read Act4 as a technical manual, you will miss the point.  
Read it as the _philosophical spine_ of the system.

* * *

**2. Act4 assumes you already understand PSC**
----------------------------------------------

PSC is the anchor.  
Act4 does not re‑explain PSC; it uses PSC as the lens through which every argument is made.

If you do not understand PSC, Act4 will feel abstract.  
If you do, Act4 becomes obvious.

* * *

**3. Each chapter is a structural invariant, not an opinion**
-------------------------------------------------------------

Act4 is organized as a sequence of invariants:

* PSC must be preserved
* deterministic formats must be used
* XML must be the canonical medium
* regeneration must be stable across languages
* long‑term reproducibility must be guaranteed
* HCMD must avoid unstable loops
* agentic systems must be contrasted with deterministic systems

These are not preferences.  
They are **requirements** for a system that claims to be reproducible across time, languages, and implementations.

* * *

**4. Read Act4 as a chain, not a list**
---------------------------------------

Each chapter depends on the previous one.

* If PSC is the spine,
* then deterministic formats are mandatory.
* If deterministic formats are mandatory,
* then XML is the only viable medium.
* If XML is the medium,
* then regeneration becomes a structural invariant.
* If regeneration is invariant,
* then long‑term reproducibility is possible.
* If reproducibility is possible,
* then HCMD can be contrasted with agentic systems.

Act4 is not modular.  
It is cumulative.

* * *

**5. Act4 is the philosophical contract of HCMD**
-------------------------------------------------

By the end of Act4, the reader should understand:

* why HCMD cannot use probabilistic formats
* why HCMD cannot rely on implicit behavior
* why HCMD cannot allow unstable loops
* why HCMD cannot be “agentic” in the modern sense
* why HCMD must remain deterministic even when implemented in multiple languages

Act4 is the part of HCMD that says:

**“If you break these rules, you are no longer doing HCMD.”**

* * *

**6. How to read Act4 effectively**
-----------------------------------

* Read slowly.
* Treat each chapter as a structural claim.
* Ask yourself:  
  **“If this invariant were removed, what would collapse?”**
* Notice how each chapter reinforces the others.
* Do not skim.
* Do not treat it as commentary.
* Treat it as the _constitution_ of HCMD.

* * *

**7. What Act4 is preparing you for**
-------------------------------------

Act4 sets the stage for the later Acts, where HCMD becomes:

* implementable
* verifiable
* regenerable
* cross‑language stable
* long‑term reproducible

Act4 is the hinge between philosophy and engineering.
