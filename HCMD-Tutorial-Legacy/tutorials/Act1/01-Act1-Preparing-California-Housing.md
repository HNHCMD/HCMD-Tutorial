# 01 — Act 1: Preparing the California Housing Dataset

Act I begins with a simple human goal:

> “Prepare the California Housing dataset for modeling.”

This is intentionally ordinary.  
HCMD does not require exotic goals.  
It requires clarity.

The purpose of this chapter is to show how a plain request becomes:

- an internal semantic interpretation  
- the emergence of the STS  
- the first movement of the HCMD loop  

You will not write code here.  
You will watch meaning form.

---

## The Human Goal

The human provides a short, natural request:

> “I want to prepare the California Housing dataset for modeling.”

This is the only explicit instruction.  
Everything else emerges from the assistant’s interpretation.

The goal contains no structure, no tasks, no implementation details.  
It is intentionally underspecified.

HCMD begins here.

---

## The Assistant’s Semantic Interpretation

Before any tasks appear, the assistant forms an internal interpretation:

- What does “prepare” mean in this context?  
- What does “California Housing dataset” refer to?  
- What does “for modeling” imply?  
- What assumptions are safe?  
- What assumptions must be avoided?  

This interpretation is **implicit**.  
You never write it.  
You never see it directly.

But you see its effects.

The assistant begins to surface clarifying questions, constraints, and structural expectations.  
This is the **Meaning Layer** at work — the internal semantic grounding (AML) beneath the STS.

---

## The First Refinement

From the single human sentence, the assistant begins to articulate:

- the nature of the dataset  
- the likely preprocessing steps  
- the modeling context  
- the boundaries of the task  
- the missing information  

This is not yet the STS.  
It is the **pre‑STS semantic field** — the assistant’s attempt to understand the goal without committing to tasks.

The human responds with clarifications.  
The assistant refines its interpretation.

This back‑and‑forth is the ignition of the HCMD loop.

---

## A Note on Skills (Huts)

As the interpretation stabilizes, certain ideas begin to cluster:

- dataset loading  
- validation  
- cleaning  
- feature preparation  

These clusters are early **Skills** (or **Huts**) — small, reusable units of meaning that will later become stable structural elements.

Act I does not name or formalize these Skills.  
It simply lets you **see them forming** as the STS approaches determinism.

---

## Why This Matters

HCMD does not begin with tasks.  
It begins with meaning.

Act I shows that the assistant cannot produce a deterministic STS until:

- the goal is understood  
- the assumptions are surfaced  
- the ambiguities are resolved  
- the scope is stable  

This chapter demonstrates that process.
